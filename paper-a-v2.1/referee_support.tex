\section{Purpose and the exact review question}

This document accompanies \textit{Reconstructing Unions of Subgroups from
Cyclic Autocorrelation}. It identifies the logical dependencies of the
principal claims, the exact role of each computation, and the commands
needed to reproduce the supplied finite evidence. The manuscript contains
the mathematical proofs; this guide does not replace a missing argument
with a repository reference. The supplied source and data are sufficient
for the review procedures below without access to research conversations
or a private GitHub account.

The primary question is whether, for every fixed modulus $N$ and every
specified union of subgroups $A\subseteq\Z/N\Z$, the stated Fourier graph
classifies \emph{all} subgroup unions $B$ satisfying
\[
 C_A(t)=C_B(t)\qquad\text{for every individually labeled }t\in\Z/N\Z.
\]
The separate modulus procedure asks whether any distinct pair exists at
that modulus. It is exhaustive and may require exponential resources.
Neither statement is a concise arithmetic description of all rigid moduli.

\subsection*{Review status}
The preparation included source reconstruction, adversarial mathematical
checks, separately written implementations of selected finite checks, and
replays of the original construction programs. The parallel audit tasks
shared model family, parent context, workspace and source material. They
are \emph{not} independent external refereeing. No principal theorem is
claimed to have been formalized or checked in Lean.

This revision responds to the supplied Grok report and the subsequent
\textit{Holds with fixes} report summary. The separate referee-response
document records the resolution of each reported issue. Theorem 10.4
retains its original hypotheses: the added calculation identifies exactly
why a nontrivial residual character has a local shell factor $-1$.

Within the stated scope, the preparation audits found no unresolved
analytic gap in the principal printed proofs. The numerical constructions
retain explicit finite arithmetic dependencies. The symbolic terminal-grid
result retains an exhaustive implementation dependency. These are the
objects to scrutinize, not reasons to presume correctness from a successful
program exit or an earlier research-status label.

\section{Adversarial targets in the universal theorems}

\subsection{Graph classification and rank}
The delicate direction is \emph{sufficiency}: a permitted sign choice must
give an actual subgroup union. Checking only the original target's signs
would not establish this. A referee should verify the following chain.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Adjacent conductor congruences imply every \emph{mixed}
finite-difference numerator is divisible by its subgroup order. The
argument groups that numerator along each coordinate and then uses
coprimality of the primary factors. No omitted integrality condition
should be supplied by an implicit numerical test.
\item Sign changes constant on the prescribed components preserve these
congruences, including pairs with a zero Fourier entry. The graph uses
only nonzero coefficients; a zero cannot introduce a fictitious sign bit.
\item Root preservation and Parseval give
\[
 \sum_x b(x)=|A|,\qquad \sum_x b(x)^2=|A|.
\]
Once $b(x)$ is integral, each $b(x)(b(x)-1)$ is nonnegative, so their
zero sum forces binary values. This also excludes negative integers.
\item Pure-prime coefficients are nonnegative for subgroup unions because
each primary fiber is empty or a subgroup. Preserving these coefficients
preserves the one-coordinate marginals.
\item The marginal--autocorrelation defect is a sum of nonnegative terms
proportional to $b_s(y)(1-b_r(y))$ for $r>s$. The target's zero defect
therefore forces the reconstructed binary profile to increase in
valuation coordinates, equivalently to decrease in element-order
coordinates. This is the actual proof of divisor closure.
\item Different component choices change a nonzero Fourier coefficient,
so the reconstruction is injective; every mate yields such a choice,
so it is exhaustive. The empty set and $N=1$ must remain covered.
\end{enumerate}

For the rank formulation, the matrix has rows $e_u-e_v$ for edges and
$e_b$ for anchors. Its kernel is constant on components and zero on
anchored components. The component-indicator basis, not rational nullity
alone, proves the $2^{\text{nullity}}$ count. An arbitrary rational matrix
need not have that many binary kernel vectors. The claimed arithmetic
operation count assumes an explicit conductor/profile grid and a supplied
factorization; it is not a polynomial bit-time result for a short
generator list.

\subsection{Independent modulus procedure}
Every monomial records the \emph{entire} squared conductor signature of an
actual binary divisor ideal. At cut $d$, with coefficient counts $a_v,b_v$,
\[
 K_d=\sum_v a_vb_v\ge0,
 \qquad 4K_d=\|P_d^++P_d^-\|^2-\|P_d^+-P_d^-\|^2.
\]
Nonnegativity comes from the first expression, not a general assertion
about differences of squared norms. A positive term must retain an ideal
on each side of the cut, which guarantees distinctness.

The transfer recurrence represents every nested primary flag exactly
once. Squaring its exponent vector is an \emph{additive, nonmultiplicative}
operation and must occur after adding all linear Fourier contributions.
Squaring individual factors early would lose mixed terms. The recurrence
does not relax binary membership or downward closure. Its exponential
state growth is an explicit limitation, not a missing proof obligation.

\section{Arithmetic proofs and the symbolic terminal certificate}

\subsection{The analytic family}
For common dyadic fibers, challenge the claim that a fixed odd conductor
can have at most one odd exact-size coefficient difference. The incoming
zero component of the difference/sum pair acquires a fixed 2-adic
valuation and cannot return to zero through larger divisible increments.
At a minimal unequal nonempty shell, the selected odd Fourier row sees
exactly its corners modulo two and gives two such odd coefficients, a
contradiction. Mixed Fourier coefficients are not assumed nonnegative.

In the primary common-zero lemma, the normalized sum lies in
$\Z[1/2]$. Inversion modulo $p^{j+1}$ is legitimate because the residual
order and $2$ are units modulo the odd prime $p$. The three changing-shell
residues are $p^j$, $-p^j$, and $p^j-2p^b$ for $b<j$. Verify the
exceptional $p=3$ comparison: it must force the absence of larger
disputes and then reduce to the separately proved one-size case. A proof
only for $p\ge5$ would not justify the stated theorem.

For $2^ep^aqrs$, aggregate equality of complementary residual-shell
masses supplies fixed signs because a discrepancy cannot reverse sign
along the primary axis. Verify that inference before the single-rank
argument. The final contradiction uses the independently established
balance of each exact dyadic size. No five-factor terminal theorem or
finite exponent-grid premise is needed for this analytic family.

The conductor convention is a particular adversarial target. On
$C_{q_1}\times C_{q_2}\times C_{q_3}$, a character of conductor $c$
has local nonzero-shell sums $x_i=q_i-1$ if $q_i\nmid c$ and
$x_i=-1$ if $q_i\mid c$. Its kernel exponents satisfy
$c=\prod_iq_i^{1-h_i}$. Thus $c>1$ forces some $h_i=0$ and
some $x_i=-1$. The all-positive evaluation is exactly conductor one.
Lemma 10.3 explicitly excludes a common zero set consisting only of
conductor one, including its exceptional prime-$3$ branch. The two
reported cancellation examples vanish only at that trivial conductor;
the exact controls below illustrate this distinction without replacing
the analytic proof.

\subsection{What terminal feasibility means}
The terminal interpolation problem asks for a \emph{real radial} $g$ with
$g=|d|$ on the upward closure $U$ of the signed support and Fourier
support inside the zero set $Z$ of $\widehat d$. It is not a binary-set
existence criterion. Its incidence equivalence is exact:
\[
 \frac{b_v}{K}\prod_i p_i^{v_i}
    =\sum_{h\in Z}M_{vh}t_h\quad(v\in U),\qquad
 M_{vh}=\prod_i(\ind_{h_i=v_i}-\ind_{h_i=v_i-1}).
\]
Check the top boundary in the inverse-column difference and the
normalization $t_h=\widehat g(h)/K$. Sufficiency follows by triangular
inversion on the upward-closed region. The conversion from an actual
least disputed class to a balanced downset difference requires its
order-convex, mutually incomparable sign supports. Excluding every such
interpolant rules out a pair; finding one does not construct a pair.

\subsection{The finite implementation obligation}
The $p^2q^2rs$ certificate ranges over six placements of the squared axes
among increasing primes. Each has 36 cells and 11,194,933 two-sided
signed patterns up to global sign. The larger replay has 24 ordered
exponent grids. These are \emph{symbolic grids for all prime values}, not
24 numerical prime tuples.

Challenge completeness of downset enumeration, lossless balanced-ternary
encoding, and each conservative rejection rule. Possible Fourier zeros
are retained as a superset; exclusion with that superset is safe. For
$p_i=3+2i+g_0+\cdots+g_i$, the gaps are nonnegative and even. A nonzero
weak-sign polynomial or an odd constant on even gaps gives the stated
nonvanishing test. Cube and integer left-null tests must use the
prescribed upward region. Modular factor rejection must use only known
units. The array bound is $36\cdot13^6<2^{31}$; nullspaces and
factorizations use exact arithmetic.

\section{Adversarial targets in the constructions}

\subsection{H100: from a signed profile to actual sets}
The section lemma is the bridge from interpolation to homometry. Check
that the proposed difference is spectator-constant and that the
spectator average of the proposed sum is the annihilating profile.
Convolving difference with sum then gives zero at every labeled shift.
Equal populations, marginal cancellation, or an annihilating rational
vector alone would not suffice.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact donor data.} Verify hidden-shell masks, totient
populations, signed-mask relations, and incidence minors $193$ and $317$.
The omitted final equation in each overdetermined packet system must
follow from the printed linear relation, not a silent solver choice.
\item \textbf{All-character annihilation.} Check each local tensor
identity and every moving-rank correction. A balanced factor can
annihilate a term, but multiplying by an arbitrary rank function need
not preserve that property.
\item \textbf{Bounds and denominators.} Keep the rational and blend
errors separate before summing them. The independently reconstructed
total H100 error divided by the step is approximately $0.2539299597$,
strictly below $2/7$ by exact rational comparison. Decimal displays are
not the certification.
\item \textbf{Binary realization.} Every specified section is a
subgroup union. Every divisor comparison, including ordinary/fine tag
comparisons, respects nesting. Equal-rank comparable core orders must
receive identical deterministic decoding inputs.
\item \textbf{Universal decoder.} The interval and parity arguments
must cover every permitted grid target. Replaying 435 or 2,175 selected
targets is a regression test, not a substitute for that proof.
\item \textbf{Nontriviality.} Check a genuinely differing boundary
fiber and the translation argument. A distinct subgroup-union mate is
not manufactured by an unnoticed symmetry outside the stated class.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{H100 height 3360 and the H99 refinement}
At level $\ell$, set $b=R-\ell$. The fine-grid precision becomes
$D_\ell=A-b$, and its baseline must also be truncated to those
$D_\ell$ positions. At the next level, each old minimum equals its
previous maximum and the new slot was previously empty. This proves
all-choice containment; monotonicity of arbitrary greedy digit strings
is not assumed.

The H99 construction removes one damping block, adds spectator guard
$65537$, and uses the supplied 504-slot ordering. The modulus is
\[
 N_{99}=2^{3408}\,\frac{65537M_{100}}
 {2101327^2\,4415573059603}.
\]
It has 99 distinct odd prime divisors and 8,017 bits. This is not a
divisor of H100, because it introduces a new prime. The height-3360
H100 refinement, in contrast, has the identical odd part.

The precision statement is about the scaled profile $Kw$ and the actual
physical target $Kw/2$, not necessarily $w$ itself. Multiplication by
$K$ and the final blend clear the relevant odd denominators. Verify
absolute precision $3401$ before reducing it by $84-\ell$. The endpoint
inequalities certify range containment; the denominator argument places
every required target on the dyadic grid. For these fixed parameters the
endpoints themselves are on that grid. The exact combined error is below
$339/1000<10/27$.
None of these constructions asserts a minimum prime count or height.

\subsection*{How to compare a replay}
Compare integer counts, rational numerators/denominators, parameter
maps, successful assertions and empty residuals. Do not expect elapsed
times, Python build strings or hashes of newly generated report bytes to
match. The inputs themselves must match the supplied pinned data.
Fractions are checked exactly; decimal convenience fields need not be
used in any proof or comparison.

The archived original and independent implementations reduce the risk of
a shared coding error, but neither makes the proof infallible. Some
independent checks use the same mathematical formulas and fixed arrays.
A valuable referee contribution would be a separate implementation of
the exhaustive grid enumerator or a small independently checked kernel
for its rejection certificates.

The guide's preparation audits are retained as source notes. In
particular, the graph audit identifies the integral/binary/monotonic
converse; the rigidity audit isolates the prime-$3$ case; the terminal
cross-audit reconstructs the scaling and upward-region inversion; and
the H99 audit distinguishes $Kw$ precision from an unsupported precision
claim about $w$.

\section{Unresolved questions and requested referee response}

\subsection{Items not certified by this version}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The exact formulations and proofs of the two unrecovered source
notes, their compactness and complexity claims, and their reported
191/2,741/73 computational counts remain unverified. Their absence is
a provenance limitation, not a proof that either mathematical claim is
false. Neither is a premise of the printed replacements.
\item Exhaustive symbolic implementation correctness is a distinct
verification obligation even after a complete successful replay. The
terminal-grid result is kept \computed for that reason. The analytic
$2^ep^aqrs$ theorem does not depend on this enumeration.
\item No principal theorem has a supplied replayable Lean
formalization. An earlier ``Lean status'' report is not a Lean proof,
and no successful proof-assistant build is claimed here.
\item This edition does not claim the broader linked localization,
finiteness or squarefree-construction theorems unless their complete
arguments are explicitly printed. In particular it makes no general
$\Omega(M)\le5$ theorem or full 196-prime construction claim. The
squarefree appendix proves only its stated local mechanism.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mathematical questions that remain open}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A useful necessary-and-sufficient prime-factor description of rigid
moduli that avoids exhaustive ideal enumeration.
\item Identification of the divisibility-minimal nonrigid moduli and the
minimum odd-prime count. The 99-prime construction supplies an upper
bound for some obstruction; it does not establish minimality.
\item The actual threshold $h(M)$ for general odd $M$, including whether
every $4M$ is rigid. Only the explicitly restricted height-two family
is proved in that direction.
\item The full arbitrary-exponent four-primary-axis problem beyond the
analytic and computed families printed here, and practical bounds for
compressed generator-based certificates.
\end{enumerate}

These are unresolved research objectives, not hidden assumptions.

\subsection{How to report a potential gap}
For each principal theorem, please distinguish correctness, exposition,
computational completeness and novelty. A useful adverse report should
name the exact statement and first unsupported inference; preserve all
its original hypotheses; and supply either a counterexample satisfying
those hypotheses or a precise missing lemma. A marginal collision or
rationally feasible terminal interpolant is not a homometric divisor-ideal
pair. A purported counterexample must have binary downward-closed
membership and identical autocorrelation at every labeled shift.

The most valuable first checks are the graph's monotonicity converse,
the common-zero argument at $p=3$, the all-choice decoder nesting and
scaled denominator budget, and exhaustive completeness of the symbolic
grid program. A polished presentation, a successful regression suite, or
agreement among related AI reviewers should not replace those checks.
