# A Level 1 counterexample on 100 distinct odd primes

**Theorem PPI-H100.** There are two different unions of subgroups of the same finite cyclic group that are not translates and have equal autocorrelation at every labeled shift. The modulus has exactly 100 distinct odd prime factors. Its 2-adic valuation is 3446. The factored modulus, exact rational parameters, and finite lists specifying the construction are included in the repository. [Modulus and parameters](../experiments/ppi_100_certificate.json) · [Ordinary slot order](../experiments/ppi_100_slot_order.json) · [Exact verifier](../experiments/ppi_100_prime_pair.py)

Write the group as $H\times S$, with coprime cyclic factors. An order shell consists of all elements of one order. A downset of orders under divisibility describes a union of subgroups, since the subgroup generated by an element consists of exactly the shells whose orders divide its order. Below, a section means a subset of the spectator group $S$ above an element of $H$.

## 1. The signed difference and its interpolating function

Use these factors of the odd group $H$.

| Factor | Explicit signed difference | Distinct primes |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Upper donor | The hidden axes $5^2,11^2,13,17$ and the 16 signed coatom fibers in PPI-UP1, followed by the three singleton-shell pairs $(776561,776563),(881,883),(29,31)$ | 26 |
| Lower donor | The 14 signed singleton shells in the array LOWER of PPI-WF1 | 14 |
| Modular helpers | The PPI-UP1 four upper coatoms and four lower singleton shells, each with signs $+,+,-,-$ | 8 |
| Damping | For each of the 20 values $p$ in PAD_PRIMES take $C_{p^2}\times C_q$, $q=p(p-1)+1$. Give order $p^2$ sign $+1$ and order $q$ sign $-1$ | 40 |

On a local factor the difference is zero off the specified signed orders. Multiply all local differences to define $e$ on $H$. The signed coatom data, including every primary exponent and hidden fiber, are the exact input of the [upper donor certificate](../experiments/ppi_unbalanced_pair.py). The lower and helper lists are fixed by the [lower data](../experiments/ppi_weighted_fibers.py) and [helper formulas](../experiments/ppi_modular_pair.py). The product support is nonempty and order-convex, and comparable support points have the same sign. Indeed this holds on each local factor by its antichain of arms and the hidden upsets on the coatoms. Let $D,U,P$ denote the indicators of its lower closure, upper closure and support, so $DU=P$. The remaining orders are called free.

We recall the interpolation result being applied, including its parameter dependence. Use exactly the individual and dyadic witnesses of [PPI-UP1](PPI_UNBALANCED_PAIR.md), with the 20 damping factors above. The proof there is local in each factor and its estimates are stated for a variable number $t$ of damping factors. Its spectral ingredients are as follows.

* On the upper donor the specified 15 character masks and the trivial character are zeros of the signed coatom transform. The incidence equations give a rational upper interpolant. On each added pair the fully nontrivial packet annihilates the singleton-shell difference. The three product corrections of PPI-UP2 give an annihilator $u$ agreeing with the support on its upper closure, with bound $U_0<31$.
* The 13 lower masks and the trivial character give an annihilator equal to support on the lower closure, with bound $L_0<2.447$. The two modular helpers provide paired dyadic witnesses on the respective closures. The lower helper also has the packet approximant $1-\alpha T$ to its upper closure, with error at most $\eta=1/(\min q_{\rm lower}-1)$ as in the [mixed-slot interpolation proof](PPI_MIXED_SLOTS_PAIR.md).
* Each damping factor has mean-zero discrepancy, since its two nonzero shells each have mass $p(p-1)$. For any binary local target subtracting its constant approximant $1/2$ leaves absolute error $1/2$. Its rank is $1-D_i+U_i$, bounded by two. With the PPI-UP1 fixed ranks the full rank $r=\sum r_i$ is weakly increasing, and $R=46+2(20)=86$. At comparable orders of equal rank every local closure indicator and every quotient activity used by the witnesses is unchanged. The PPI-UP1 ranks also record the activity of every axis of the added pairs and modular helpers. Thus the interpolation and the decoder below agree at such orders.

For definiteness, given positive step $s$ and intercept $b$ from the manifest, use $c=2(n+b+Rs)/K$ and $\delta=2s/K$, with $K,n$ as in section 2. Use the [weighted-fiber rational profile](PPI_WEIGHTED_FIBER_PAIR.md#annihilation-and-precision)
\[
 w_r=c+(2-c)F(D)-cF(U)+(c-1)F(P)
  -\delta\sum_j\bigl[r_j-G_j(D)-G_j(U)+G_j(P)\bigr].
\]
Here $F(X)=X-\prod_i(X_i-a_i)$ with local target $X_i$ and the stationary approximants of PPI-UP1. For $D,U,P$ respectively these are $(u,1,u)$ on the upper donor, $(1,l,l)$ on the lower donor, and midpoints on helpers and damping factors except for the lower-helper upper-closure approximant above. $G_j(X)$ uses the same subtraction for the product $r_jX$ with the [moving-block rules](PPI_UNBALANCED_PAIR.md#interpolation-with-the-nonzero-mean-blocks). In particular the moving upper donor and its upper helper are grouped for $r_jU$ and use $r_j\otimes1$. The balanced helper kills that term even though the three added pairs individually have nonzero mean. Other stationary helpers in moving terms use midpoints, and a moving helper or damping factor uses zero.

Each surviving term in a product subtraction has an annihilating factor. The rank terms alone have an independent balanced factor. Exactness on the lower and upper closures follows from the respective local interpolation identities. Hence $e*w_r=0$ and the forced values are $2-P$ on $D$ and $P$ on $U$. The PPI-UP1 estimate on free orders is
\[
 |w_r-(c-\delta r)|\le 2^{-20}
 \left[\frac{(1+U_0)(3+L_0)}4+(1+L_0)\eta+
       \tfrac32 R\delta(1+U_0)(1+L_0)\right].
\]
The verifier recomputes the individual bounds from the character data and checks that the right side divided by $\delta$ is below $254/1000$.

Let $E$ be the odd part of the product of the original two individual clearing denominators. Define $\lambda=E\lfloor 2^{367}/E\rfloor/2^{367}$. Use $w=\lambda w_r+(1-\lambda)w_0$, where $w_0=2F(D)-F(P)$ uses the paired upper and lower dyadic witnesses, extended over the partial pairs as in PPI-UP1, and ordinary midpoints on damping factors. The two profiles have the same forced values and both annihilate $e$. The [denominator and norm proof](PPI_WEIGHTED_FIBER_PAIR.md#annihilation-and-precision) applies since $K$ is an integer, $Kc,K\delta$ are dyadic and $0<c-R\delta<c<2$. In particular $Kw$ is dyadic. With $m=7/2$, the verifier bounds the change from the blend by $\delta/(33m)$. Thus every free order satisfies $|w-(c-\delta r)|<\delta/m$. All the denominators needed after the further division for fine decoding are bounded in section 4. The certificate uses exact elimination, integer shell masses and Pocklington tests for the large helper primes.

## 2. The twelve spectator primes

The odd part of $S$ projects onto a tag quotient of order
\[
 Q=3^5\,7^3\,19^2\,23^2\,37^2\,41^2\,43\,47\,71\,83\,113\,149 .
\]
Use this displayed prime order for tuples of projected order exponents. The guards are $3,7,113$. The control primes are $23,37,41$. The six remaining primes $19,43,47,71,83,149$ carry additional kernel powers $(4,3,3,3,3,2)$ respectively, of product $B$. The control primes each carry one additional kernel power, of product $C=34891$. Thus $|S|=QBC\,2^{3446}$. The map on odd parts is reduction onto $C_Q$ with kernel of order $BC$. Common prime factors of the kernel and quotient are included in these exponents.

For tag $v$ let $V(v)=\prod_i\varphi(p_i^{v_i})$, with $\varphi(1)=1$. The tag $g$ having full exponents on the three guards and zero elsewhere has mass $G=5334336=64h$, where $h=83349=3^5 7^3$. Measure section sizes in units $hC\,2^{3446}$. The full normalized mass is the integer $K=QB/h$. An unrestricted odd preimage above tag $v$ has normalized mass $V(v)B/h$ at full dyadic height.

A tag is early if either the 3 or 7 exponent is below its maximum. It is late if those two are full and the 113 exponent is below its maximum. In each class consider the tags with $h\mid V(v)$. Take the 214 heaviest early tags and 400 heaviest late tags, resolving a tie by decreasing lexicographic order. Call these the ordinary slots. Fill every other early or late tag completely as the background. All tags with full guards outside $g$ are empty. Above $g$ use the fine slots below. The normalized background mass is
\[
 n=\frac Bh\sum_{v\text{ in background}} V(v).
\]
The verifier enumerates every tag and every immediate divisor, verifies the divisibility of this sum by $h$, and checks that the background is downward closed and contains every strict divisor of $g$. There are 124416 tags, of which 121210 belong to the background.

### Ordinary slots

Use the permutation in the slot-order file. Its integers are zero-based indices into the tag tuples in increasing lexicographic order, so the last exponent changes fastest. Pair successive entries as groups numbered from zero. The verifier checks that this is precisely the chosen set, early first, and that every ordinary immediate divisor of an ordinary slot occurs earlier. Each immediate divisor either belongs to the background or is such an earlier slot. This also controls non-immediate divisors by induction.

Put $W_i=V(v_i)B/h$. Take $H_0=\lfloor 99 W_{\min,\mathrm{early}}/100\rfloor$. Let $\beta_i$ be the unique integer power of two for which $H_0/2<W_i\beta_i\le H_0$. Write $z_i=W_i\beta_i$. For a used ordinary slot take the full odd preimage, with the dyadic subgroup at the height specified in section 4. Thus $\beta_i$ is its base relative height, possibly greater than one before the level scaling of a late slot.

The first step on a prime axis multiplies shell mass by $p-1$ and each subsequent step multiplies it by $p$. Hence strict divisibility $v_i<v_j$ implies $W_j\ge 2W_i$. The common normalization interval then gives $\beta_j\le\beta_i/2$. This allows the ordinary slots to be comparable even when their digits are selected independently.

### Fine slots

Above $g$, the variable and control coordinates project to zero, so their allowed elements lie in the respective primary kernels. Use the 3840 possible variable kernel shell tuples $v$, with each exponent running from zero to its additional kernel exponent. Set
\[
 V_v=G\prod_{p\ {\rm variable}}\varphi(p^{v_p}),\qquad
 d_v=\prod_{p\ {\rm control}}p^{v_p(V_v)},\qquad
 u_v=\operatorname{oddpart}(V_v)/(h d_v),
\]
where $v_p(V_v)$ in the middle expression is the prime valuation. The checker verifies $d_v\mid C$ for every $v$. Use the subgroup of index $d_v$ of the control kernel at this shell. If the nominal fine height is $a$, take actual dyadic height $aM/2^{v_2(V_v)}$ with $M=64$. The physical odd mass is $V_vC/d_v$, so the normalized contribution is exactly $M u_v a$.

Sort the slots by increasing $(u_v,\sum v_p,v)$ lexicographically. A strict divisor precedes its multiple, its control index divides that of its multiple, and its dyadic rescaling factor is at least that of its multiple. Indeed the shell mass divides the successor mass, and removing the control factors preserves divisibility of the odd weights. Ties in weight are resolved by increasing degree. The verifier checks these properties at every immediate divisor. Use the first $A=3353$ slots and leave later ones empty. The first eight odd weights equal one. The exact tail bound is $\sum_{j=9}^A u_j2^{-j}<1/4$, using one-based fine indices.

## 3. Nested sections and digit weights

At level $l=R-r$, use ordinary groups $k=0,\ldots,R+159$ inclusive. Later groups are empty. For $k<l$ freeze the group at doubled base heights. For $k\ge l$ use base heights divided by $2^{k-l}$, with an independent option to double each height. Early doubled base heights are at most one. Late groups begin at index 107 and have $k-l\ge21$ even at $l=86$. The checker verifies that every late doubled height after this scaling is at most one.

At each level the multiplier common to a group before digit selection decreases weakly with its group index, with the frozen groups already doubled. Together with $\beta_j\le\beta_i/2$ on strict comparisons, the smallest allowed height at a divisor is at least the largest allowed at a multiple. The empty tail comes last. Every choice on the ordinary slots therefore respects divisibility.

For fine slot $j$ use nominal height $2^{l-R-j}$ with an independent option to double. Along strict comparisons the index increases by at least one, which absorbs the optional doubling. The rescaling and control subgroup inclusion proved in section 2 preserve the containment in the odd and dyadic coordinates. Dividing an element while staying over $g$ preserves these restrictions or goes to an earlier variable shell. Projecting to a strict divisor of $g$ leads to the full background. Ordinary tags miss a full guard and cannot lie above $g$. Any ordinary tag below $g$ was excluded by the strict-divisor check. Thus every section is a downset. All heights exist on the cyclic dyadic factor. The largest required fine exponent is $3353+86+7=3446$. The ordinary shifts and depths are smaller. Heights of zero specify the empty section.

Increasing $l$ by one makes the minimum allowed height at least the previous maximum on each variable slot, or keeps an already frozen slot at its fixed value. The control restrictions do not depend on $l$. Consequently **every** decoded section at the higher level contains **every** decoded section at the preceding level.

## 4. Exact mass at each free order

The manifest gives $s,b$ as integers divided by explicitly stated powers of two. They can also be regenerated. Round twice the mean of the first 107 group sums of $z_i$ to the nearest multiple of $E/2^{161}$ to obtain $s$. Let $C_l$ be the ordinary minimum including frozen groups, and $J_l$ the sum of the two base weights in group $l$. Write $\lambda_l=2^{l-R}$. The number $b$ is the midpoint of the intersection over $l$ of the intervals
\[
 \left[C_l+5M\lambda_l/4-ls+s/m,\quad
       C_l+5M\lambda_l/4-ls+J_l+H_0-s/m\right].
\]
The verifier regenerates both manifest rationals and checks the interval intersection is nonempty.

On a free order of rank $r$, the desired normalized section mass $Kw/2$ belongs to $[n+b+ls-s/m,n+b+ls+s/m]$ by section 1. Write $T=\sum_{j=1}^A u_j2^{-j}$. From the desired mass subtract $n+C_l+M\lambda_l T+M\lambda_l/4$. At each of the 87 levels the verifier checks, at both endpoints, that the result lies in $[0,J_l+H_0]$. The subtraction is affine in the desired mass, so this holds throughout the interval.

Visit the unfrozen ordinary groups in order and double each digit whose increment fits the remaining number. A skipped digit leaves a residual smaller than that digit. Taking both digits subtracts $J_l$ from an input at most $J_l+H_0$. Either way the first pair leaves at most $H_0$. At depth $j\ge1$ the input is at most $2H_0/2^j$, the individual digits are at most $H_0/2^j$, and their sum exceeds $H_0/2^j$. The same argument bounds the output by $H_0/2^j$. The final depth is $159+R-l$, and $H_0/2^{159}\le M/2$. Adding back the reserve $M\lambda_l/4$ leaves the needed fine increment in $[M\lambda_l/4,3M\lambda_l/4]$.

After division by $M\lambda_l$, the fine increment lies on the grid $2^{-A}\mathbb Z$. We detail where the precision is checked. In the notation of the [shared template verifier](../experiments/ppi_mixed_slots.py), $d_y$ bounds the combined dyadic exponents of the individual donors including the three partial-pair roundings, and $e_u,e_l$ bound the paired dyadic anchors. The base coefficient $Kc$ has exponent 177, $K\delta$ needs at most 161, the flat lower-helper coefficient needs 2003, and the final blend costs 367. The product expansion in section 1 introduces at most 20 ordinary midpoint bits, plus the two helper midpoint bits. No term uses either individual donor twice. The verifier takes the conservative bound
\[
 367+20+\max(177+d_y+2003+3,\ 161+d_y,\ e_u+e_l)+6
     +\operatorname{bitlength}(M)
\]
for the scaled profile precision including division by $2M$. The numerator of the blend clears $E$. Ordinary normalized weights have at most $\operatorname{bitlength}(K)$ fractional bits at base height and at most a further $159+R$ from depth. The checker takes the maximum with $\operatorname{bitlength}(K)+159+R+5+\operatorname{bitlength}(M)$, obtaining $A=3353$. Division by $\lambda_l$ multiplies by an integer, and the normalized fine baseline $T$ is already on the grid. These estimates cover every free order.

To decode the fine increment $y$ now scaled to $[1/4,3/4]$, start with $2^Ay$. For $j=A,\ldots,9$ select the digit when the current integer is odd, subtract $u_j$ if selected, and divide by two. Oddness of $u_j$ makes each division exact. The total selectable tail is less than $1/4$, so the resulting integer is nonnegative and less than $2^8$. Its eight binary digits select the first eight weights, all of which equal one. Doubling exactly those fine heights adds the prescribed mass. The uniform interval and grid proof covers all interior grid points. As a separate arithmetic check, the verifier reconstructs integer masses in units of $2^{-3446}$ for 435 test targets across the levels.

## 5. The sets and the all-shifts identity

Define $A$ and $B'$ by their sections over $H$. On $D\setminus P$ give both all of $S$, and on $U\setminus P$ give both the empty set. On signed support give $A$ all of $S$ at sign $+1$ and the empty section at sign $-1$, with the opposite choice for $B'$. On a free order give both the section just decoded. These rules and the two input lists determine both sets without a choice of witnesses or a search.

Order-convexity and sign consistency on comparable support orders give the forced inclusions. A free order below a support point would belong to $D$, and one above would belong to $U$. Comparable free orders use nested sections since the level decreases as order increases. Equal-rank comparable orders use the same $w$ and the same deterministic decoding. Thus both sets are downsets of orders and are subgroup unions.

Write $f=1_A$ and $g'=1_{B'}$. Then $f-g'=e(x)$ throughout the spectator fiber above $x$ and $\sum_{s\in S}(f+g')(x,s)=|S|w(x)$. Both indicators are even. With unnormalized convolution, for **each** labeled shift $(z,y)\in H\times S$,
\[
 C_A(z,y)-C_{B'}(z,y)
 =((f-g')*(f+g'))(z,y)
 =|S|\sum_{x\in H} e(x)w(z-x)=0.
\]
The full/empty choices make the section-size profiles distinct. Both profiles are even on odd $H$. If translation by $a$ in $H$ carried one profile to the other, evenness would make $2a$ a period. In a group of odd order $a$ is an integer multiple of $2a$, hence is also a period. The profiles would then coincide. A translation of the two sets would induce just such a translation of profiles, so none exists.

All 48 fixed primes, the 40 damping primes and the twelve spectator primes are disjoint. The additional spectator kernel powers only increase exponents of these same twelve primes. The verifier compares the resulting prime-power map to the manifest and separately checks the listed damping and spectator primes by trial division. This proves the asserted count and the theorem.
