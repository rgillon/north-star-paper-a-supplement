\section*{2\quad Scope of the results included}

\textbf{Universal set-level classification.} For a nonempty subgroup union $A$, construct the graph on its nonzero integer conductor coefficients. Adjacent congruences determine its edges; the root and pure prime-power conductors anchor components. If $u$ components contain no anchor, there are exactly $2^u$ subgroup-union mates, including $A$. Each selected component is negated and the inverse transform explicitly reconstructs its mate. The proof covers Fourier zeros, repeated prime factors, every dyadic height, and the separate empty-set case.

\textbf{Conductor-grid rank.} The matrix has an incidence row for each graph edge and a coordinate row for each anchor. Its kernel has the unanchored-component indicators as a basis. Its binary kernel therefore is a cube, which justifies the exact power-of-two count. The stated $O(\omega(N)\tau(N))$ arithmetic-operation bound assumes an explicit valuation-grid profile and a supplied prime factorization. It is not a polynomial bit-time bound for a succinct generator list.

\textbf{Universal modulus decision.} A polynomial records the complete squared conductor spectrum of each actual divisor ideal. Membership cuts separate unequal ideals. A nonnegative coefficient moment is positive precisely when a spectrum occurs on both sides of a cut, and retained generating data recover a witnessing pair. The factor recurrence preserves the full unsquared spectrum until its contributions have been added, so mixed Fourier terms are retained. The algorithm is finite and exhaustive; its potentially exponential enumeration is explicit.

\textbf{Analytic rigidity.} Complete arguments cover odd and twice-odd orders, prime powers, a restricted $4M$ family, two arbitrary odd primary axes, the family $2^e p^a q^b r$, and the family $2^e p^a qrs$. In the last two formulas the displayed primes are distinct and odd and the displayed positive exponents are arbitrary. The $4M$ theorem assumes at most one prime divisor of $M$ is congruent to $3\pmod4$.

\textbf{Symbolic computation.} The $2^e p^2q^2rs$ result is identified as \textbf{COMPUTED}, with its exact terminal reduction and ordered-grid certificate. The grids represent exponent and prime-order patterns, not a finite selection of numerical prime tuples. Their arithmetic exclusions are uniform in the prime values. The exhaustive implementation remains an explicit verification dependency; the referee guide records the current replay status.

\textbf{Constructive nonrigidity.} The complete 100-prime interpolation, nested-section realization, and all-shift homometry argument are retained. Rank-dependent fine-slot truncation gives height 3360 for the same odd part. A separate refinement gives
\[
 N_{99}=2^{3408}\frac{65537M_{100}}{2101327^2\cdot4415573059603},
\]
with 99 distinct odd prime divisors. Both refinements are \textbf{PROVED with finite exact certificates}. None is asserted minimal. A local squarefree-block and tensor-damping appendix preserves a distinct useful method without asserting the larger squarefree construction.

\subsection*{What still warrants independent scrutiny}

No unresolved gap was identified in the included principal analytic proofs during this preparation audit and the targeted referee recheck. In particular, the conductor objection to Theorem 10.4 does not leave a missing premise under the printed definitions. This is an assessment of the checked arguments, not a substitute for independent refereeing. The most consequential checks are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item that adjacent conductor congruences suffice for integral inversion and that the marginal-defect identity forces divisor closure after an allowed sign flip;
\item that the primary-axis common-zero argument includes the exceptional prime $3$ and every allowed exponent;
\item that the terminal enumerator covers every admissible signed pattern and implements every symbolic exclusion correctly;
\item that all construction data satisfy the asserted exact inequalities, and that rank-dependent truncation preserves the grid and all-choice nesting for every target, rather than only tested targets.
\end{enumerate}

For the 99-prime refinement, the dyadic assertion concerns $Kw$, not necessarily $w$ itself. Endpoint inequalities certify range containment; the separate denominator proof places every actual target on the required grid. For the fixed supplied parameters the endpoints themselves also lie on that grid. These distinctions are retained explicitly.

\section*{4\quad Open mathematics and the limits of verification}

\subsection*{Questions that remain open}

\begin{itemize}
\item A necessary-and-sufficient structural description of rigid moduli in terms of prime factors and exponents that avoids exhaustive enumeration of divisor ideals.
\item The least nonrigid modulus, the least possible number of odd prime divisors, and the divisibility-minimal nonrigid moduli. The 99-prime construction provides an upper bound, not a minimum.
\item Rigidity at unrestricted $N=4M$ with $M$ odd. The proved congruence-restricted family does not settle this question.
\item The first nonrigid dyadic height for a specified odd part, and the attainable values of that height. Heights 3360 and 3408 are sufficient construction bounds.
\item The general four-primary-axis problem with arbitrary prime-power exponents, beyond the analytic families and explicitly recorded symbolic certificates.
\item Effective compression bounds for generator-based certificates and a direct arithmetic characterization of realizable unanchored components.
\end{itemize}

Algorithmic completeness resolves a finite question for each input: every mate of a supplied set can be recovered, and every factored modulus can be decided by the stated exhaustive procedure. It does not replace these open questions about the structure of the infinite family of moduli.

\subsection*{Evidence produced during preparation}

The preparation included adversarial mathematical reconstruction and exact computational checks. An independent graph implementation checked all 8,182 radial profiles and 465 subgroup unions over $1\leq N\leq60$, including complete direct autocorrelation checks for every nonempty radial profile at $N\leq30$. An independent cut-moment implementation checked 1,567 ideals over 85 moduli, with 503 cut moments and 2,248 transfer comparisons. These finite regressions check formulas and implementations; the universal theorems rest on the supplied proofs.

The original H100 certificate and separate arithmetic and decoder audits were replayed. A new implementation, importing no construction-verifier module and using the previously independently checked H100 donor rationals as data, verified the changed arithmetic for both refinements. It checked 174 H100 and 170 H99 endpoint inequalities and reconstructed 435 and 425 actual grid targets, respectively. The proof for every target uses interval coverage, the absolute precision bound, and the parity decoder; sample reconstructions are supplementary evidence.

The complete ordered-grid replay finished during this preparation: all 24 ordered grids passed with no unresolved patterns, and all six two-squared-axis grids reproduced every recorded exclusion count. Its elapsed time was 1,039.8 seconds. The referee-support document and dated machine-readable record give the exact commands and per-grid totals.
